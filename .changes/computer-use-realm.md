### Added

- **`@reticlehq/server` — a desktop application can join the bridge with no SDK in it.** A realm that reads an application through the operating system joins as a peer, and the tools for a web page then work on it. The Harness (`reticle_verify { action: "explore" }`) gets a prompt for a desktop application: it sends the model to a file, a setting and the clipboard, not to a link or a request. The realm package (`@reticlehq/computer-use`, macOS) is in the repository and is not published yet. It reads an application through the accessibility tree, acts in the background, and, when a caller asks with `args.foreground: true`, uses the real pointer for a double-click, a hover, a drag and the menu bar. For an application with no elements it gives a picture of the window to `reticle_screenshot` and acts at a point of it. A text that takes no click, as the display of Calculator, is visible when the system gives the area that holds it as the answer of a hit test. The join sets the accessibility switch for a Chromium browser too, with the second switch that Chrome needs before a press reaches its page. A ref goes with a field that the application moves in its tree when the field takes a value, as the search field of Dictionary. The three buttons at the top of a window have names (`Close window`, `Minimize window`, `Zoom window`), from what the system says they are. An item of a list (a group with no name, beside twins of the same shape) is known by its words and not by its place, so a row that comes above it does not give its ref to another row. It reads and scrolls a page in Safari and in Chrome; a page with no scroll bar is scrolled with a key. When a desktop application and an app with the SDK are both connected, `reticle_session { action: "list" }` gives a `realms` field that says which session to use for what. Its `samePage` names a window of the desktop session that shows the page of an SDK session, matched by title. A click or a hover at a point of the picture goes to that point: the middle of the element of the ref can be under another view, as the middle of Dia's window is under its sidebar. A point on a part of a window that gives no element, as the pinned tabs of Dia, is accepted when the window in front at that point is the application's own. A radio button and a menu item with the value 1 say `checked`, so the current tab of a sidebar and the current profile of a switcher are known. A press with no key in `args.text` is refused with a message that says where the key goes, in place of the helper's "unknown-key". `reticle_screenshot { screen: true }` takes the whole display that holds a desktop application, with every application on it: an overlay of another application, or a dialog of the system, is in the picture. A click at a point of that picture goes to that point of the screen. A session with no desktop realm says that it cannot take the screen, and gives no picture of a page in its place. The HUD of a desktop session shows the person's turn: `reticle_session { action: "yield", mode: "ask", note }` opens it with "Your turn: <note>", and its Pause button becomes "Done". "Done" reaches the agent as the person's message (`reticle_messages`), for a 2FA code or a captcha in the middle of a task. The Dock is visible: its application gives the size 0 and has no window, and that size hid every item in it. A list of a Chromium page that gives no visible rows (the list of servers in Discord) shows its rows. `scroll` with `args.foreground: true` turns the real wheel over the element, for a list of a web view that has no scroll bar and takes no key. While a desktop session is live, the pointer of the HUD is on the screen: black with an edge of orange and blue that moves round it. It goes to each control the realm presses, and to the point of a click at a point of a picture. A button under an empty layer that clicks go through (Discord keeps one over its whole window for popups) is not refused as covered: Chromium names that layer in its hit test. A button behind the backdrop of an open dialog is still refused. The windows of the HUD are no longer named as the window over a target. A button behind a popup that has no mark of a dialog is refused too: the popup is drawn after its backdrop. A new action, `window`, arranges the window of an element: `{ arrange: "left" | "right" | "zoom" | "minimize" | "fullscreen" | "restore" }` or `{ frame: { x, y, width, height } }`. It puts two windows side by side, fills the screen, sends a window to the Dock and brings it back (from the application element, as from its tile in the Dock). The answer gives the window after the change, with `minimized` and `fullscreen`, and the action fails when the window is not as asked: an application with no tile in the Dock says yes to minimize and keeps its window on the screen. A page refuses `window`. A point of a picture is now `args.point` (it was `args.at`), and `at` and `to` are fractions of the element's box in a desktop session too, as in a page. A drag ends on another element with `toRef` (as in a page), at a point of the picture with `toPoint`, or at a point of its own box with `to`; it starts at `point` or `at`, so a drag can go from one point of the picture to another, as a window is moved in Mission Control. `scroll` with `point` and `foreground` turns the wheel at that point. A page refuses `point` and `toPoint` and says why: it ignored them before, and acted at the middle of the element. In a desktop session, an `at` or a `to` outside 0 to 1 is refused and names `point` or `toPoint`: a model that thinks in pixels of the screenshot sent `at: { x: 600, y: 400 }`, and it was a click at the corner of the element that reported done. While computer use is live, the HUD of the desktop shows each act that the agent does in a browser tab, as a row with the tab's session (`s1 → click button "Save"`): a desktop session now sees the feed of every project, and it showed only its own actions before. The desktops of macOS are reachable as a person reaches them: Control+Up, Control+Down, Control+Left and Control+Right go to the system even though no application has the keyboard (the Dock, which holds Mission Control, never has it), and Mission Control then shows its Spaces Bar with a button for each desktop and "add desktop". A key to an application as a whole no longer takes the reach test of a click. A key with modifiers sent in the foreground now lets the modifiers go after it: Control, Fn and the number pad stayed held after Control+Up, and every key typed on the machine after it was a Control key. The items at the right of the menu bar (Control Centre, Wi-Fi, Focus) are read when their application is not in front: they were dropped as a menu bar that is not on the screen. So an agent opens Control Centre with a click and turns Stage Manager on or off with `check` or `uncheck` on its checkbox. A baseline of `reticle_screenshot` from a desktop session goes to `.reticle/visual/x-computer-use/`: it was saved with the pages, so a desktop `home` and a page `home` were one file. The column view of Finder is read: a column gives its list only as its contents, and every file of it was missing. A long list gives the items on the screen and says how many it left out, as a table does (a column of 958 files was read whole). A click on the words of an item in a list chooses the item. The files of an open stack of the Dock (Downloads) are read: the list of the stack gives no place at all (0,0,0,0), and it hid every file in it. An action that can turn off Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, Airplane Mode, Ethernet or the network switch (an `uncheck` or a click on its checkbox) is refused unless `args.confirmDangerous` is `true`: Control Centre is readable now, and off, these cut the agent's own link or the person's keyboard and mouse. To turn one on is not refused. A scroll with neither `args.dy` nor `args.fraction` is refused and says what to send: the wheel turned 0 lines, or the bar was set to its own place, and the answer said that it scrolled. A list of a page whose items are out of view says how many on its own line (`[not shown: 290]`): the channels of Discord showed ten items and said nothing, so an agent took them for the whole list. `scroll` with `fraction` and `foreground` turns the wheel until the list stops moving, and says `atEnd`: one turn of 40 lines did not reach the end of 300 channels. The pointer of the HUD follows a drag: it goes to where the drag starts and then to where it ends. It jumped to the end before a drag to a place of the element, and it stayed at the start of a drag from one point of a picture to another.

### Changed

- **`@reticlehq/engine` — a signal that an observer saw is evidence for a session with no SDK.** The rule `signal-without-consequence` is for a signal that the application sends about itself. A session of a peer realm has no SDK, so each of its signals is a thing the realm saw from outside: a file that was written, a setting that changed. An action whose only effect is such a signal now gets `yes`. A session with an SDK is judged as before.
