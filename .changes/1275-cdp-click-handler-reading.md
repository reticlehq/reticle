### Fixed

- **`@reticlehq/server`: a plain navigation link is exempted only on a reading that the link has no handler, taken through CDP.** The destructive guard reads a control's `href` along with its text, which is right for a button whose label is an icon: its `formAction` is the only place it says what it does. On an anchor the href is an ADDRESS, and addresses carry the pattern's words, so `Orders & invoices` pointing at `/billing/payment` was blocked for the `payment` in the URL, and the way past a block is `confirmDangerous: true` passed reflexively. The exemption needs the one fact a page cannot produce for itself. A page sees the DOM and any framework's props, and a listener bound with `addEventListener` is in neither, so an absence a page reports is an absence nobody observed. A driver holding a CDP session can observe it: `DOMDebugger.getEventListeners` lists real listeners, including `addEventListener` ones. The reading is taken by the element's ref, through the page's own registry, so the node read is the node the guard will act on rather than whatever sits at a coordinate. It walks the element, every ancestor in the COMPOSED tree (stepping to a shadow host at a shadow root, because a click is composed and crosses that boundary), `document` and `window`, because a delegated handler on any of them runs on the click. `false` exempts, `true` keeps the block, and `undefined` (no CDP session, an unresolvable ref, or a chain that could not be read) keeps the block too. Where no CDP session exists the link stays blocked, so a page with no driver behaves exactly as it did before. **The exemption applies only when native input drives the tab**, because the reading needs a CDP session: a default synthetic click still refuses the reported link, and that is the next piece rather than something this change claims to have done. Closes [#1275](https://github.com/reticlehq/reticle/issues/1275).

- **`@reticlehq/browser`: a framework probe can no longer report a handler absent.** `hasClickHandler` on `ReticleAdapter` answered `false` when the framework's props held no handler, and that is what let a link wired with `ref.addEventListener` or a document-level listener take the exemption. It now answers `true` or `undefined`, never `false`: props are the only thing a fibre exposes, so the probe can prove a handler exists and never that one is absent. `INSPECT` carries the field only when a handler was actually found, so a descriptor can keep a block and can never grant the exemption on its own.

- **`@reticlehq/server`: the non-GET marker reaches the native path.** `data-method`, `data-turbo-method`, `data-remote` and the htmx verbs render an `<a href>` that reads as a GET and intercept the click in script to issue a DELETE. `inspect` reports `nonGetMarker` beside `isAnchor`, `hasClickHandler` and `insideForm`, and the guard refuses on it.
